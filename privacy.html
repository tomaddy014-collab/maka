Privacy: where student information goes

Teachers, school leaders and whānau are right to ask what happens to student work once it goes into Māka. This page answers that in plain language, based on how Māka is actually built.

Last updated 13 October 2026. This describes how the app works; it is not a legal document and it is not legal advice. It is written with the principles of the New Zealand Privacy Act 2020 in mind (be open about what is collected, collect no more than is needed, keep it safe, let it be deleted). That is the aim, not a promise of legal compliance.

The short version

  • Marking happens at Google. When you mark, the student work you upload is sent from your browser to Google’s Gemini service, using your own Gemini API key. Māka cannot mark work without sending it there.
  • Signed-in teachers have results saved. If you are signed in, marked results and a PDF copy of each piece of work are saved to your Māka account, which is stored with a service called Supabase. If you are not signed in, nothing is saved to that database.
  • Your account, plus students with their code. Only your account can open your saved work in Māka. A student can see their own results only if they have the login code you gave them. The person who runs Māka can technically reach what is stored in the database, as with any hosted service.
  • Deleting removes the data it names. Deleting a result, a session or your account removes the saved results and the stored PDF copies. Two things stay by design or limitation: removing a student from the class list keeps their marking history, and your sign-in login itself can only be removed by the person who runs Māka. Details are below.
  • Google’s and Supabase’s own terms also apply. What they do with data depends on your Gemini plan and Supabase’s settings and terms. Māka cannot promise anything about that. Check your school’s policy before uploading student work.

Use a key you created just for Māka, and if you ever doubt it, delete it and create a new one in Google AI Studio.

What is sent to Google Gemini

Marking is done by Google’s Gemini model, not by Māka. Your browser sends the request straight to Google using your own Gemini API key. Māka does not have a server in the middle that passes it on; the pages themselves are hosted on Netlify, which is not part of that request.

What is included in those requests

  • The student’s work, converted to a PDF in your browser. If the student wrote their name on it, that goes too, and Māka asks Gemini to read the name off the work so it can label the result.
  • The file name, which is often a student’s name.
  • The learning area, level, task, rubric and your writing-style text.
  • When marking writing, a second “moderation” request, which sends the work again together with the first round of marking.
  • For test marking, the test paper you upload.
  • For a resubmission, the earlier version of the work (fetched from your stored copy) and the earlier feedback.
  • For report comments and weekly posts: the student’s name (first name only when generating for the whole class; full name from a student hub), pronouns, scores, strengths and next steps from past results, and your writing-style text. The he/she/they setting is used only to choose pronouns. Year level, login codes, email addresses and passwords are not included.

What Google does with it depends on your plan. Google’s terms for how Gemini API inputs are handled can differ depending on your key’s plan and billing setup. Māka cannot see or change that. Check Google’s current Gemini API terms before uploading student work, and ask your school if you are unsure. Where Google processes and keeps data is also set by Google, not Māka.

Who can see student work

  • You, signed in to your teacher account. Māka is designed so each teacher’s saved data is limited to their own account. You see only your own class, sessions and results.
  • A student, with their login code. On the student login page a student enters the code you gave them and sees their own name, year level and results: scores, criteria feedback, strengths, next steps and session details. The student portal does not show the stored PDF of their work, and does not show the class list or other students’ names.
  • Google and Supabase, as the services that process and store the data (see the sections above and below).
  • The person who runs Māka. Whoever administers the Māka database can technically read what is stored in it, as with any hosted app. Māka does not include advertising or analytics scripts in its own pages.

Two things to know about student login codes.

First, the portal finds a student’s results by their full name in the saved file name (matched as whole words). Older files saved with a first name only are matched by first name only when no other student in your class shares that first name, and only as a whole word, so Jo never matches Joshua. If two students share a first name and a file only has that first name, it is not shown to either of them. This matching lives in the Supabase database function, so it only applies once the updated supabase-student-portal.sql has been run on the project — ask the person who runs Māka to confirm. Using full names in file names (the Class-list assignment does this automatically) keeps results attached to the right student.

Second, a code does not expire, so treat it like a password. New codes are 8 characters long (older 6-character codes still work until you regenerate them). You can generate a new code for a student in the Class tab at any time. Māka does not add its own limit on repeated guesses.

Deleting data

What the buttons currently do:

  • Removing one student’s result (from the Mark tab after the 4-second undo, or from a past session in History): deletes the saved result row and the stored PDF copy.
  • Deleting a whole session (History): removes every result in the session, their stored PDF copies, and then the session itself (after the 4-second undo window).
  • Removing a student from the class list (Class tab or student hub): deletes the class-list entry (name, year level, setting, login code, so the student’s code stops working). Their results, stored PDFs and any saved report comments stay.
  • Saved report comments have no delete button of their own; they are removed by Delete account.
  • Delete account (Settings): removes your sessions, results, stored PDFs, class list, saved rubrics, report comments and your profile record (email, display name, API key, writing style), clears the cached results in your browser, and signs you out. It does not remove your sign-in login itself — only the person who runs Māka can do that. If anything fails part-way you are told, and you can run it again.
  • Data already sent to Google cannot be deleted from Māka. That is governed by Google’s terms.

If you need a student’s data fully removed, delete their results (and the student from the class list) as above. To remove your sign-in login as well, ask the person who runs Māka (contact details below).

What stays in your browser

Māka saves a few things on the device you use. None of it is sent anywhere by these features.

  • Your latest marking results (maka-results): file names, scores, feedback and short quotes from the work, kept so a refresh does not lose your marking. Kept for up to 24 hours, replaced when you mark again, and removed by the Clear button. Signing out now clears it too, and Clear removes it at any time.
  • Preferences: light or dark mode, your last learning area and level, Classroom-comment options and print layout.
  • Your sign-in session, stored by the Supabase library until you sign out.
  • A student’s login code is held for that browser tab only and is cleared on log out or when the tab closes.
  • Offline copy of the site: a service worker keeps the page files and icons so Māka can open offline. It does not store student data.

Māka’s pages load fonts from Google Fonts and code libraries from the jsDelivr network, so those services receive the normal details any website visit sends, such as your IP address.

Your responsibilities as a teacher

  • Check your school’s policy on AI tools and on sharing student work with outside services, and follow it. If your school requires consent or notice for whānau, get it before you upload.
  • Use as little identifying information as you need. Consider first names or initials in file names rather than full names, and avoid uploading work that contains sensitive personal details.
  • Use an API key created for Māka, and keep it private.
  • Treat student login codes like passwords, and press Clear and sign out on shared devices.
  • Review Māka’s marks and comments before they reach students or whānau. They are a first draft from an AI.
